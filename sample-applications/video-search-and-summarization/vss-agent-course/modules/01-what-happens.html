PLAIN ENGLISH

Define a job called streamChat. It takes your message, plus a way to report each piece of the reply as it arrives.

Knock on the door at /api/chat and wait. That address is the agent.

We are sending something, not just asking for a page. That is what POST means.

Tell the agent the parcel is written in JSON, so it knows how to unwrap it.

Pack your message, the video you attached, and the recent conversation into that parcel.

Include a rip-cord. If you hit Stop, this is what cancels the whole thing mid-flight.

Send it.

POST Sending data to a server, rather than just requesting a page. Posting a letter, not reading a noticeboard.
JSON A plain-text way of writing structured data that both sides agree on. Almost every service on the internet talks in it.
/api/chat An endpoint — one specific address on the agent that handles one specific job.

Why the answer arrives in dribs and drabs

The agent does not wait until it has the whole answer. It streams each word the moment the model produces it.

1
Without streaming: 15 seconds of nothing

The screen sits blank, then the entire paragraph appears at once. It feels broken, so people press the button again.

2
With streaming: first word in 6 seconds

Same total time. But you can see it thinking, so you wait instead of giving up.

This was a real bug in this codebase. The switch that turns streaming on was left at its default — off — so one giant message arrived after a long silence. Turning it on changed one reply from 1 chunk into 269.

🕐
Perceived speed is a feature

Nothing got faster. Only the order changed. A large slice of performance work in real products is like this — not making the wait shorter, but making it visible.
