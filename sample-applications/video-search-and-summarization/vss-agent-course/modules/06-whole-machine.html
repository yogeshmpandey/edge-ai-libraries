The question that decides the next version

Look again at the agent's territory. Four of those five boxes hold state or do work the video system arguably should have done.

📏
A measure you can use anywhere

The amount of state a client has to keep is a measure of how much the service is not doing. When you find yourself instructing an AI assistant to "remember which ones we already handled", stop — that is usually a sign the thing you are calling should have told you.

What you now know how to ask for

Six modules, and the point of all of them was to make these sentences sayable.

"add a capability" Not "add a feature" — one class, and the tool list, the menu and the wiring follow from it.
"degrade, do not fail" This optional service being down should make the feature worse, not absent.
"set temperature to 0 for tool decisions" Stop the choice of whether to act being sampled at random.
"verify before showing" Check the top results against the source before presenting them as answers.
"give unknown its own state" Do not fold "not checked" into "failed".
"stream the response" Send it as it is produced rather than in one lump at the end.
🗣️
Vocabulary is the leverage

You do not need to write this code. You need to be able to name what you want precisely enough that an assistant builds the right thing — and to notice when it has not.

That is the machine

A chat box, six job adverts, a standard container, a fact-checker, and a lot of careful behaviour for when the things around it misbehave.

None of it is magic. It is a model making one narrow decision, wrapped in ordinary software that does the actual work — and that is true of every agent you will meet.
